False or misleading 10

Wrong on the facts, or built on a real fact then distorted past accuracy.

"We have 19.2 trillion dollars being spent in our country"

Versions of this claim have been repeatedly rated false. The White House's own website listed $9.6 trillion — about half his figure — and even that total includes aspirational multi-year pledges and future product purchases rather than realized capital investment. For scale, annual US GDP is roughly $30 trillion.

Verdict: False.

"I'm number one on TikTok... 425 billion views"

He has roughly 16.7M TikTok followers; Taylor Swift has roughly 33.5M. He is the most-followed world leader on the platform. The 425 billion figure counts all-time views of the #Trump hashtag — including neutral and hostile videos posted by other users — not views of his own account.

Verdict: False as stated; true in the narrower "among world leaders" sense.

"Crime is down 88%" in DC / "150 people were killed a year"

DC homicides are down roughly 37–46% in 2026 — a real and steep decline, but not 88%. The baseline is also wrong: DC recorded 187 homicides in 2024 and 274 in 2023, not 150. Homicides had already fallen 32% in 2024, well before the National Guard deployment.

Verdict: False on the number; the underlying trend is real.

"Toyota just announced the biggest car plant in the world. They're leaving Mexico. Tariffs did that."

Three separate errors: - It is a $3.6B expansion — a second assembly line at Toyota's existing San Antonio plant, adding ~150,000 units of capacity and ~2,000 jobs. Not the world's largest plant. - Toyota is not leaving Mexico. It is shifting Tacoma output from the older Tijuana facility while continuing production at its newer Guanajuato plant. - Toyota attributed the move to "multi-decade decisions based on broader strategic goals," and CNN reports automakers remain broadly resistant to reshoring after a year of tariffs.

Verdict: False.

Iran — "It's all going extremely well. Don't believe the fake news."

As of the date of the speech, the US–Iran conflict was escalating, not resolving. Iran was still striking tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and US targets in the region, oil had topped $100/barrel, and a Houthi Red Sea attack marked a fresh escalation days earlier. His specific counts — 250 jets, 159 boats — are not independently confirmed.

Verdict: Contradicted by contemporaneous reporting.

Newsom "can't read a speech... he's a low IQ person... various mental incapacities"

Newsom's remark referred to dyslexia, which he has discussed publicly for years and described as a "superpower." Dyslexia has no bearing on general intelligence, per the Yale Center for Dyslexia and Creativity. The related claim that Newsom "gets sued for being a racist" turned up no lawsuit — only criticism of remarks at an Atlanta book-tour event. The claim that he "admitted Kamala was smarter than him" could not be verified.

Verdict: Misrepresents a disclosed reading disorder as low intelligence.

cnn.comTrump repeatedly suggests Newsom can’t be president because he has dyslexiaRead →

"The single best recruiting... in the history of our country"

All five services hit FY2026 active-duty goals months early — genuinely strong. But Pentagon officials describe it as the best in about 15 years, not ever; raw enlistment was far higher in the draft era. The "two years ago" framing is also off: FY2023 was the weak year, and by FY2024 all services were already meeting their goals.

Verdict: Exaggerated. Real achievement, wrong superlative.

"I built it for $2.9 billion less than the Federal Reserve building, which is a lot smaller"

The Old Post Office renovation cost roughly $200M. The Fed's Eccles renovation is roughly $2.5B (up from a $1.9B baseline). That is a gap of about $2.3B, not $2.9B. The Old Post Office is roughly 400,000 sq ft, so "a lot smaller" is doubtful for a two-building Federal Reserve complex.

Verdict: Inflated.

Bud Light / Dylan Mulvaney — "$35 million" then "$35 billion"

He cited two different figures and neither matches. Anheuser-Busch InBev shed roughly $4–6B in market value in the immediate aftermath (April 2023) and roughly $27B by June 2023. It was also not a "commercial" — it was a sponsored social-media post featuring a personalized can.

Verdict: Overstated.

Skipping the dinner — "First time that ever happened"

Nixon skipped in 1972 after the Pentagon Papers fight. Reagan skipped in 1981 while recovering from being shot, phoning in from the hospital. Trump was the first to skip an entire term's worth of dinners, not the first to skip.
